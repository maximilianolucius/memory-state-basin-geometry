# 3. Search B — delay/hereditary basin and headpoint analogues

## 3.1 Strongest adjacent prior: Szaksz–Stepan–Habib 2024

**B. Szaksz; G. Stepan; G. Habib.**  
“Dynamical integrity estimation in time delayed systems: A rapid iterative algorithm.”  
*Journal of Sound and Vibration* 571, 118045 (2024).  
DOI **10.1016/j.jsv.2023.118045**.

This remains the single closest conceptual prior found.

The paper states explicitly that in a DDE:

> convergence depends on the entire initial function, not only on its headpoint.

It defines basin/integrity through the headpoint of a **constrained family of initial histories** and considers:

- constant histories;
- linear histories;
- jump histories;
- free-vibration histories.

Its Figure 3 explicitly compares **different initial functions with the same headpoint**.

Even closer, their iterative algorithm can choose as a new initial-condition headpoint a physical point lying on a previously simulated trajectory. The authors explicitly note that the new integration begins from a constrained initial history, whereas the corresponding segment of the old trajectory was generated by the entire nonlinear DDE history, so the new trajectory can differ from the old one.

That is very close in spirit to the project's distinction:

[
	ext{inherited continuation state}
quad	ext{vs.}quad
	ext{restart/cold start at same physical point}.
]

### Why it does not kill TARGET-A20

The 2024 paper does **not** prove that:

1. the old physically inherited state is in basin (A_+);
2. the restarted constrained history with the same headpoint is in basin (A_-);
3. (A_+

4. the pair is physically reachable in the Caputo continuation-state architecture.

Their discussion says the restarted trajectory will differ and is expected to diverge with high probability in the algorithmic setting. It is an algorithmic basin/integrity construction, not a theorem of opposite basin membership for the same headpoint.

Therefore DDE headpoint geometry is a strong conceptual antecedent but not a routine theorem implication for TARGET-A20.

**M. Mrozek.**  
“Rigorous Error Analysis of Numerical Algorithms via Symbolic Computations.”  
*Journal of Symbolic Computation* 22(4), 435–458 (1996).  
DOI **10.1006/jsco.1996.0061**.

Mrozek explicitly presents rigorous symbolic/a-priori floating-point error bounds as an **alternative to interval arithmetic**, developed for computer-assisted dynamical-systems proofs.

Therefore binary64 layers are not automatically non-rigorous if every operation and elementary-function error is mathematically bounded.

## 7.3 Actual weakness of the TASK-0006 arithmetic model

TASK-0006 is strong where it uses:

- Arb ball arithmetic;
- exact rational parameters;
- explicit Higham-style operation/summation error bounds;
- positive-term outward slack.

The likely referee vulnerability is the stated assumption that libm functions

[
	exttt{pow}, 	exttt{expm1}, 	exttt{log1p}, 	exttt{gamma}

are accurate to “a few ulp.”

That is an **assumption about an implementation**, not an independently enclosed mathematical bound unless tied to a specific libm/version/platform and verified.

but may object to calling the numerical layer simply “rigorous interval validation” or “fully validated computation.”

They may ask:

- can those calls be enclosed by Arb/MPFR or directed rounding?
