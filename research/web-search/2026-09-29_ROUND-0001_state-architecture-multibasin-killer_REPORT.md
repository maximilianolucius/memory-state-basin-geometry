# Q1 — Exact Caputo memory-state architecture

## 2. Primary source: Doan & Kloeden (2021)

**Thai Son Doan; Peter E. Kloeden.** “Semi-Dynamical Systems Generated by Autonomous Caputo Fractional Differential Equations.” *Vietnam Journal of Mathematics* 49 (2021), 1305–1315. DOI: **10.1007/s10013-020-00464-6**.

### 2.1 Equation and basic hypotheses

The paper studies
[
{}^C D_{0+}^{\alpha}x(t)=g(x(t)),\qquad 0<\alpha<1,
]
in (mathbb R^d), and initially assumes (g:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^d) is globally Lipschitz.

The standard point-IVP is represented by
[
x(t)=x_0+\frac1{\Gamma(\alpha)}
\int_0^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}g(x(s))\,ds.
]

The generalized singular Volterra equation is
[
x_f(t)=f(t)+\int_0^t a(t,s)g(x_f(s))\,ds,
\qquad
a(t,s)=\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}.
]

The standard Caputo point-IVP corresponds exactly to the **constant input**
[
f_0(t)\equiv x_0.
]

### 2.2 State space and topology

The semigroup state space is
[
\mathfrak C=C(\mathbb R_+,\mathbb R^d)
]
with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets.

The paper metrizes this topology by
[
\rho(f,h)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}\rho_n(f,h),
\qquad
\rho_n(f,h)=
\frac{\sup_{t\in[0,n]}\|f(t)-h(t)\|}
{1+\sup_{t\in[0,n]}\|f(t)-h(t)\|}.
]

For project notation, a source-faithful choice is therefore
[
\mathcal H_\alpha:=\mathfrak C,
]
with this compact-open metric/topology. The underlying set does not depend on (alpha); the transfer operator does.

**Terminology caution.** The source calls (mathfrak C) a “Banach space” while simultaneously specifying the compact-open topology by the above metric. For the project, avoid relying on a global normed-space claim here; say **continuous-function state space with compact-open topology induced by (ho)** unless a later proof specifically requires a Banach norm.

### 2.3 Transfer/continuation operator

For (	au\ge0),
[
(T_\tau f)(\theta)
=
f(\tau+\theta)
+
\int_0^\tau
a(\tau+\theta,s)g(x_f(s))\,ds,
\qquad \theta\ge0.
]

Doan–Kloeden Theorem 4 proves, under global Lipschitz (g), that the generalized integral equation generates a **semigroup of continuous operators**
[
\{T_\tau:\tau\ge0\}
]
on (mathfrak C), with
[
T_{\sigma+\tau}=T_\sigma T_\tau.
]

### 2.4 Continuity and solution dependence

The same paper proves:

- unique solution (x_f) on every bounded interval for every continuous input (f) under global Lipschitz (g) (Theorem 1);
- continuous dependence of (x_f) on (f) in finite-horizon sup norm (Theorem 2);
- continuous dependence of (x_{\alpha,f}) on fractional order (alphain(0,1)) in finite-horizon sup norm (Theorem 3);
- continuity of each transfer operator (T_\tau) in the compact-open topology (Theorem 4).

Remark 1 weakens the sufficient vector-field assumption: the semigroup construction also works for an **admissible** (g) whenever the generalized Volterra equation has a globally defined unique solution for each (f\in\mathfrak C) with continuous dependence on the input.

### 2.5 Canonical physical embedding and present evaluation

Remark 3 identifies the physical solution:
[
\iota(x_0)(t):=x_0,
\qquad
e_0(f):=f(0),
]
so that
[
x(t;x_0)=e_0(T_t\iota(x_0))=(T_t\iota(x_0))(0).
]

Thus the Chief’s proposed physically reachable set
[
\mathcal R_\alpha=
\{T_t\iota(x_0):t\ge0, x_0\in X_{\rm phys}\}
]
and reachable present-state fiber
[
\mathcal F_x=e_0^{-1}(x)\cap\mathcal R_\alpha
]
are fully compatible with the published architecture.

### 2.6 Attractor confirmation

**Doan & Kloeden (2024),** “Attractors of Caputo semi-dynamical systems,” *Fractional Calculus and Applied Analysis* 27, 2305–2316, DOI **10.1007/s13540-024-00324-x**, explicitly reuses this (mathfrak C)-semigroup and proves existence of a global Caputo attractor under dissipativity plus local Lipschitz hypotheses.

The current Springer monograph **Doan, Kloeden & Tuan, _Attractors of Caputo Fractional Differential Equations_**, DOI **10.1007/978-3-032-05511-8**, has dedicated chapters on Caputo dynamical systems and Caputo semigroup attractors. Publisher metadata lists copyright 2025, with eBook publication 1 Jan 2026 and softcover publication 3 Jan 2026.

### Q1 verdict

**VERIFIED.**

The project can replace provisional (mathcal H_\alpha) language by the exact (mathfrak C) architecture above for the globally Lipschitz baseline, with later weakening only when an admissibility/global-continuation theorem is proved for the target ecological model.

# Q2 — Collision-to-multibasin prior (M1)

## 3. M1 is a standard semigroup consequence

Let
[
\mathcal B(A)=
\{\phi:\operatorname{dist}(T_t\phi,A)\to0\}.
]
For any (r\ge0), if (phi\in\mathcal B(A)), then
[
\operatorname{dist}(T_t(T_r\phi),A)
=
\operatorname{dist}(T_{t+r}\phi,A)
\to0.
]
Hence (T_r\phi\in\mathcal B(A)): the basin is positively invariant.

Therefore, if
[
e_0(T_t\iota(p))=e_0(T_s\iota(q))=x
]
and the two lifted states belong to distinct basins, both states lie in the same reachable fiber (mathcal F_x) and the fiber is multibasin.

This is a useful **reduction lemma**, but its proof is definition + semigroup property. No novelty should be attached to the abstract implication itself.

## 4. Closest hereditary/endpoint-projection prior

### 4.1 DDE history-space basin geometry

**Huang, Yang, Yi & Zou (2014),** *Journal of Differential Equations* 256, 2101–2114, DOI **10.1016/j.jde.2013.12.015**, provides rigorous basin estimates for a bistable delay equation with an Allee-type application. This confirms that history-space basin geometry is mature adjacent prior.

**Liz & Ruiz-Herrera (2015),** “Delayed Population Models with Allee Effects and Exploitation,” *Mathematical Biosciences and Engineering* 12, 83–97, DOI **10.3934/mbe.2015.12.83**, works on
[
C_+=C([-\tau,0],\mathbb R_+)
]
with the usual segment semiflow. Their Theorem 3.1 gives sufficient history sets leading to extinction versus the positive attractor. Those sufficient sets are separated by the Allee threshold in the history range, so this theorem does **not** itself produce a same-endpoint/opposite-basin pair.

### 4.2 History-parameter basin slices and Wada geometry

**Daza, Wagemakers & Sanjuán (2017),** “Wada property in systems with delay,” *Communications in Nonlinear Science and Numerical Simulation* 43, 220–226, DOI **10.1016/j.cnsns.2016.07.008**, computes basins by varying finite-dimensional families of history functions and reports Wada basin structure in the infinite-dimensional history state. It is strong prior against any broad novelty claim about “basin geometry created by memory/history.”

### 4.3 Headpoint-projected basin language

A particularly relevant adjacent paper is:

**Szaksz, Stepan & Habib (2024),** “Dynamical integrity estimation in time delayed systems: A rapid iterative algorithm,” *Journal of Sound and Vibration* 571, 118045, DOI **10.1016/j.jsv.2023.118045**.

The authors explicitly define a basin **based on the headpoint of a constrained initial history** because the true DDE phase space is infinite-dimensional. Their Figure 3 compares constant, linear, jump and free-vibration initial histories having the **same headpoint**. The paper also stresses that the basin/integrity estimate is tied to the chosen family of histories.

This is not the Chief’s theorem: their reduced basin selects a constrained history-generation rule rather than proving that two full history states with the same headpoint lie in distinct basins. But it is direct prior for the **projection/headpoint-basin viewpoint** in hereditary dynamics.

### M1 verdict

**STANDARD CONSEQUENCE.**

Residual novelty cannot be “define basins over fibers/projections” in a broad hereditary sense. It must be the exact, rigorous geometry of **physically reachable Caputo continuation states** inside a common (e_0)-fiber, preferably with opposite ecological outcomes.

# Q3 — Transversal persistence prior (M2)

## 5. What is already standard

If a finite-dimensional collision map
[
H(z;\mu)=0
]
is (C^1) in the solved variables (z), continuous in the external parameter (mu), and a suitable (d\times d) derivative minor is nonsingular at a zero, local persistence is the ordinary parameterized implicit-function mechanism.

Accordingly, the abstract IFT step in M2 should be treated as standard mathematics. The Caputo-specific burden is to verify the exact regularity of the physical observation map and robust basin membership.

## 6. Published Caputo/Volterra regularity pieces

### 6.1 Order continuity

Doan–Kloeden (2021), Theorem 3: for fixed continuous input (f) and globally Lipschitz (g), the finite-horizon solution (x_{\alpha,f}) depends **continuously** on (alpha\in(0,1)) in sup norm.

This is useful for perturbing fractional order, but it is **not differentiability in (alpha)**.

### 6.2 Parameter continuity and sensitivity

**Guo, Ma & Wu (2016),** “On sensitivity analysis of parameters for fractional differential equations with Caputo derivatives,” *Electronic Journal of Qualitative Theory of Differential Equations* 2016(118), 1–17, DOI **10.14232/ejqtde.2016.1.118**.

Relevant statements:

- Theorem 3.11: finite-horizon continuous dependence on a finite-dimensional parameter (mu) when (f(t,x,mu)) is continuous and uniformly Lipschitz in (x).
- Section 3.3: when (f), (partial_x f) and (partial_\mu f) are continuous, the solution is differentiable in (mu) locally and satisfies the associated fractional sensitivity equation.

The order (alpha) is fixed in this sensitivity derivation.

Their differentiability statement for a “restarted” problem in ((t_0,x_{t_0})) must **not** be silently identified with differentiability of the fixed-lower-terminal reachable Caputo memory-state map; the paper explicitly formulates that restarted problem without the complete inherited prehistory.

### 6.3 Generalized history initial data

**M. I. Gomoyunov (2022),** “On differentiability of solutions of fractional differential equations with respect to initial data,” *Fractional Calculus and Applied Analysis* 25, 1484–1506, DOI **10.1007/s13540-022-00072-w**, studies a generalized Caputo Cauchy datum consisting of a current time plus a preceding history and proves differentiability properties of the endpoint functional using fractional coinvariant derivatives.

This is highly relevant to a future derivative audit, but it is not an off-the-shelf theorem saying that the Chief’s specific map
[
(p,q,t,s;\theta,\alpha)\mapsto
P_{\theta,\alpha}(p,t)-P_{\theta,\alpha}(q,s)
]
is jointly (C^1) on the desired domain.

### 6.4 Nonlinear Volterra implicit-function/sensitivity prior

**M. Majewski (2017),** “Control system defined by some integral operator,” *Opuscula Mathematica* 37, 313–325, DOI **10.7494/OpMath.2017.37.2.313**, treats a nonlinear Volterra integral operator. Using a global implicit-function theorem, it proves well-posedness/robustness and continuous differentiability of the control-to-trajectory solution operator under its hypotheses.

This is strong adjacent subsumption of the **method**, though not of the Caputo singular-kernel multibasin statement.

### 6.5 Broader well-posedness in order/initial data

**K. Diethelm (2014),** “An extension of the well-posedness concept for fractional differential equations of Caputo’s type,” *Applicable Analysis* 93, 2126–2135, DOI **10.1080/00036811.2013.872776**, records continuous dependence on the given function, initial value and fractional order, and extends well-posedness to changes in the starting point.

## 7. M2 residual and required proof burden

No direct published theorem was found of the form:

> a transverse physical-state collision between standard autonomous Caputo IVPs belonging to distinct robust basins persists as a multibasin reachable present-state fiber under joint model-parameter/fractional-order perturbation.

The abstract geometry is nevertheless not new. To make M2 manuscript-eligible, the Chief should verify:

1. (t_*,s_*>0) or another regime where required solution-map time regularity is valid;
2. (C^1) regularity in whichever finite-dimensional variables are actually solved by IFT;
3. continuity of those derivatives with respect to external parameters, including (alpha), if (alpha) is varied;
4. no use of a “restart from the present point only” theorem that discards Caputo prehistory;
5. robust trapping neighborhoods (or another rigorous basin-persistence mechanism) for both asymptotic outcomes.

### M2 verdict

**ADJACENT SUBSUMPTION.**

The IFT persistence skeleton is standard; what remains potentially publishable is the Caputo-specific verification and its use to promote one certified inter-basin collision to an open family.

## 8. Strongest adjacent positive evidence

### 8.1 Fractional maps: overlapping attractors and intersecting projected trajectories

**Mark Edelman (2011),** “Fractional Standard Map: Riemann–Liouville vs. Caputo,” *Communications in Nonlinear Science and Numerical Simulation* 16(12), 4573–4580, DOI **10.1016/j.cnsns.2011.02.007**.

This is the strongest conceptual novelty threat found in the round. The published paper reports, primarily numerically, that fractional maps with memory can have intersecting trajectories and overlapping chaotic attractors; a displayed Caputo fractional standard-map case has two overlapping independent attractors.

Why it does **not** close the current target:

- it is a discrete fractional map derived from kicked dynamics, not the autonomous continuous finite-terminal Caputo ODE class used by the project;
- the highlighted overlapping-attractor regime is outside the project’s (0<\alpha<1) continuous-Caputo setup;
- the result is numerical/conjectural rather than the project’s reachable-fiber theorem;
- “overlap in a projected phase portrait” is not the same as proving two physically reachable Doan–Kloeden continuation states with identical (e_0) and distinct omega limits.

Why it matters:

> the manuscript must not claim as new the broad statement that memory can allow trajectory intersections, projected attractor overlap, or different futures despite projected-state coincidence.

(2024) explicitly discuss different history functions sharing the same headpoint and define projected/headpoint basin quantities. These are close hereditary analogues, but the searched passages did not establish the full strict same-headpoint/opposite-basin pair required here.
