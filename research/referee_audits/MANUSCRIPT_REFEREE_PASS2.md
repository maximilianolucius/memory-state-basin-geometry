## 1. Doan--Kloeden continuation-state architecture

### Source
Doan & Kloeden (2021), *Vietnam Journal of Mathematics* 49, 1305--1315.

### Source hypothesis
The global semigroup theorem on
\[
\mathfrak C=C(\mathbb R_+,\mathbb R^d)
\]
is proved under globally Lipschitz \(g\) (or, in the source's remark, for an admissible \(g\) for which the generalized Volterra problem is globally uniquely solvable with continuous input dependence).

### Manuscript model
The strong-Allee predator--prey vector field is polynomial and therefore only locally Lipschitz on \(\mathbb R^2\).

### Required fix
Do not silently apply the full-state-space theorem to the raw polynomial.

Use a standard smooth cutoff:
1. TASK-0006 + M1 place the full inherited B215 standard trajectory in a compact set.
2. X1 places every cold-start trajectory generated by the certified entry interval in a compact set.
3. The union of these sets is compact.
4. Choose \(\chi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2)\) with \(\chi=1\) on a neighborhood of that compact set and set
   \[
   \widetilde g=\chi g.
   \]
5. Then \(\widetilde g\) is globally Lipschitz.
6. By uniqueness, every load-bearing standard trajectory for \(g\) is exactly the corresponding trajectory for \(\widetilde g\).
7. The Doan--Kloeden transfer formula therefore gives the identical continuation-state orbit for every witness state used in TARGET-A20.

The basin split is independent of the choice of cutoff because both witness states and their complete semigroup orbits remain in the region where \(g=\widetilde g\).

This is a technical state-space localization, not a modification of the physical theorem.
